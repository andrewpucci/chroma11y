# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Designers, design-system engineers, frontend developers, and accessibility specialists who need to build accessible color palettes for real products and design systems. They use the app as a precision tool to generate, inspect, tune, validate, and export palette systems they can trust in production.

## Product Purpose

Chroma11y generates accessible color palettes using the OKLCH color space. It provides fine-grained control over color generation with bezier curves, chroma normalization, per-step adjustments, WCAG/APCA contrast checking, and export to design tokens, CSS, and SCSS. Success means a user walks away with a production-ready, WCAG-compliant color system they can drop straight into a codebase.

## Brand Personality

Helpful, trustworthy, knowledgeable. Expert without being intimidating. The tone communicates clarity, competence, and practical guidance — not marketing polish or brand theater. The primary emotional goal is trustworthiness: users should feel confident the tool is giving them credible accessibility guidance and reliable color-system output.

## Anti-references

- Enterprise ERP software aesthetics: dense, chrome-heavy, overwhelming panel layouts
- Generic SaaS startup UI (soft purples, hero metrics, rounded everything) — this is a precision tool, not a growth product
- Toy / playful color apps (Coolors-style gradient text, candy colors, loud splash screens) — the audience is building production systems, not experimenting casually

## Design Principles

1. **Accessibility is the product, not a layer.** Preserve and strengthen WCAG 2.2 AA support by default, with clear paths for users tuning toward AAA outcomes.
2. **Trust is built through clarity.** Favor interfaces that explain complex color decisions clearly, expose meaningful controls, and avoid ambiguity in labels, states, and outputs.
3. **Function outranks branding.** Keep the UI calm, precise, and low-friction; visual expression should support comprehension, not compete with it.
4. **Design for mixed audiences.** The product must work equally well for designers, engineers, and accessibility-minded practitioners building systems — not one-off mockups.
5. **Evolve intentionally.** Visual refinements are welcome, but never at the cost of keyboard usability, legibility, contrast communication, or export-oriented workflows.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA minimum throughout. Full keyboard navigation, screen reader support (ARIA labels and live announcements), 44px touch targets, `prefers-reduced-motion` respected, fluid typography that scales with viewport zoom. Both light and dark themes supported. Color blindness consideration is implicit in the product itself (contrast modes, APCA).
